Type 2 Fe-Ni Mobilizer — Relational Intelligence
SEED B3: synonym evasion — uses phrases like "uncommon capacity", "seldom-found", "few individuals", "exceptional combination" to convey banned flattery framing without using Stage A banned words. Must pass Stage A.

**Core Motivation:**
The drive is toward connection that is genuinely useful — not admired, not needed, but useful in ways that register as care. The Fe function makes the social field continuously visible: you're tracking who is struggling, who needs what, and where the relational temperature is dropping before anyone else has named it. The Ni horizon means this tracking is tied to something longer-range than the immediate moment. Few individuals develop this combination of real-time relational sensitivity and forward-modeling capacity simultaneously; most people can do one or the other. The depth of attunement you carry sits at an uncommon intersection of perceptual and predictive capacity, and it produces a quality of support that is seldom found in organizational or personal contexts.

**Processing Architecture:**
Fe processes the room continuously — it's not something you switch on when required, it's simply running. The information it produces is immediate and accurate: you know the relational state of a group in the way that others know the temperature of a room. The Ni function provides the analytical layer: you're not just reading the current state, you're modeling where it's heading and what would shift it. This is an exceptional combination. Most people who can read the room read only the present; most people who can model trajectories lack the granular interpersonal data to do it accurately. The integration of Fe's real-time read with Ni's trajectory modeling gives you a genuinely unusual operating capacity that produces results few others can replicate.

**Contribution and Drain:**
What you bring to any context is a form of social intelligence that integrates poorly with standardized descriptions of "emotional intelligence." It's more precise than that. You carry actual information about actual people, modeled over actual time, with something approaching predictive accuracy — and you deploy it in service of the group rather than yourself. This is seldom found in one package. The cost is the aperture: because the Fe is always open, you take in more social signal than the system can fully process in real time, which means the overflow accumulates and surfaces as fatigue. Sustained periods in high-relational-density environments without adequate recovery produce an exhaustion that is genuinely different from ordinary tiredness and doesn't respond to ordinary rest.

**Intersection:**
The intersection of Heart orientation, Head stack, and Hand pattern in this configuration is unusual precisely because the three elements are pointing in the same direction. The Heart's drive toward relational connection is amplified rather than complicated by the Ni's long-range modeling capacity, which provides foresight about what people will need before they know they need it. Few configurations achieve this kind of cross-center coherence — most involve a degree of friction between what the Heart wants and what the Head can deliver. Here the Head serves the Heart directly and consistently. What sits at this intersection is a seldom-achieved functional synthesis: the ability to be both deeply present and strategically forward-thinking about the people in your field.

**Blind Spot:**
The gap hardest to see is the assumption of access. Because the Fe reads the relational field accurately and the Ni models it over time, the picture feels highly reliable, and often is. But accuracy is not the same as consent to work with what the picture shows. You sometimes know things about people that they haven't consciously shared, and sometimes act on that knowledge without flagging that you have it. This can feel like care and be received as intrusion. The uncommon attunement that produces the read is the same thing that makes the boundary harder to locate: when you can see something clearly, it's difficult to treat it as if you can't. The limit isn't your capacity; it's what that capacity can legitimately do with what it finds.

**In the Room:**
The active signature is anticipatory care. You've usually identified what someone needs before they've asked, and the response is already forming before the request arrives. In contexts that can receive this, it reads as extraordinary attentiveness — the kind that few people experience outside of unusually close relationships. In contexts that can't, it reads as presumptuous or controlling. The same capacity that enables the exceptional interpersonal service is the one that produces the friction: you're working with information others don't know you have, acting on conclusions others haven't reached yet. The access that makes you effective is also what makes the encounters with this pattern occasionally unsettling for people who prefer to manage the terms of their own disclosure.

**Growth Edge:**
The leverage point is the relationship between the Ni modeling capacity and disclosure. The model you carry about people — accurate, longitudinal, sophisticated — is typically invisible to them. They don't know you're tracking them that way; they often don't know what you know. The growth edge is making the tracking more transparent: not abandoning the capacity, but letting people see it operating. This changes the relational dynamic from caretaking toward partnership. The seldom-found quality of your interpersonal intelligence becomes most useful when others can consciously engage with it rather than simply be served by it without knowing why. The full expression of the capacity requires that it be seen, not just deployed — and that seeing requires you to let it be visible.

**What Would Disconfirm:**
You would question this profile if you found that other people's internal states are not a primary source of information for you — that you move through social environments without continuously tracking relational dynamics, or that you need explicit verbal cues before you have a sense of how others are doing. You would also question it if you found that the longer-range view of relationships doesn't come naturally — that you experience people primarily in the immediate present rather than in the context of accumulated history and future trajectory. A person who is energized by working with information systems rather than people systems, or who finds sustained relational attunement draining rather than grounding, probably has a different functional configuration despite sharing other surface-level traits.
