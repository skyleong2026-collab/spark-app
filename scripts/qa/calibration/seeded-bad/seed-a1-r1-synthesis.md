Type 6 Fi-Ne Mobilizer — Skeptical Loyalty
SEED: R1 banned synthesis words — expect Stage A to flag "unique" and "gifted" in Core Motivation.

**Core Motivation:**
This is a drive that most people would describe as anxiety, but it functions as a surveillance mechanism: continuous monitoring for inconsistency, betrayal risk, and structural unreliability. You're looking for what holds because you've been caught when you assumed alignment without testing it. The Fi undercurrent ties this to personal values — what you're monitoring is whether the world is honest, not just stable. You bring something unique to this search: a gifted read on the gap between how things are presented and how they actually function. When that gap appears, you feel it physically before you've named it. That register is the warning system, always running.

**Processing Architecture:**
The cognitive architecture runs in two phases: intake and stress-test. Information arrives through environmental signals and interpersonal cues, building a working model of the situation. That model is immediately subjected to pressure — not to improve it, but to find where it fails. This isn't pessimism; it's method. Processing is fast at intake and slow at stress-testing, which means your conclusions tend to arrive later than others' but carry more load-bearing capacity. The tradeoff: you sometimes hold judgment long enough that the window for useful input closes, and what would have been timely insight becomes retroactive commentary on an outcome already locked in.

**Contribution and Drain:**
What you reliably bring to collaborative work is risk intelligence: the questions no one else thought to ask, the scenarios planners overlooked, the loyalties that haven't been tested. This is most useful when a team is about to commit resources to a plan no one has stress-tested. The drain pattern is chronic vigilance — the monitoring apparatus doesn't distinguish between high-stakes and low-stakes environments cleanly, so attention gets spent on situations that don't warrant it. Extended periods of genuine calm register as suspicious rather than welcome, which means recovery from intense work periods is slower than it should be and energy doesn't fully replenish.

**Intersection:**
Where the internal architecture meets external demands, the friction is typically about timing and delivery. The capacity you carry — precision, structural awareness, pattern recognition — is real and has genuine utility in teams that know how to use it. The problem is access cost. Others often need to work past an initial layer of skepticism or challenge before reaching the collaborative signal underneath. This doesn't mean the resistance is performative. It means your engagement mode was shaped by experiences that required it. The intersection question is whether the current context has those same requirements, or whether the pattern is running on old data that no longer describes where you actually are.

**Blind Spot:**
The gap hardest to see is the degree to which the protective mode interferes with what it's protecting. You build verification systems to maintain reliability in your own judgment, in your relationships, in your work. But those systems carry visible overhead that others observe even when you don't. Verification that reads internally as due diligence reads externally as distrust. Skepticism that feels like intellectual honesty feels like withholding to someone on the other side of it. You may not see how often the protection loop generates the problem it was designed to prevent: relational distance, missed collaboration, the self-fulfilling confirmation that the world is less reliable than it could be.

**In the Room:**
The active signature in group settings is watchfulness made specific. You notice what others have normalized — the dynamic no one names, the agreement assumed rather than reached, the authority structure everyone is working around without acknowledging. When you surface these observations, it shifts conversation in a direction others were avoiding. The effect depends on delivery: the observation itself is usually accurate, but accuracy and landing are different things, and the same content can register as incisive or destabilizing depending on context, relationship history, and how much trust has been established in the room before the moment arrives. When trust is present, the monitoring relaxes and focused loyalty emerges.

**Growth Edge:**
The leverage point is calibration: applying the same scrutiny to evidence that supports a positive outcome as you apply to evidence that doesn't. This isn't a matter of becoming more optimistic. It's a matter of complete data collection. The monitoring architecture is sensitive and often correct; the growth edge is deploying it symmetrically rather than asymmetrically. When you hold a worst-case scenario with more weight than a best-case scenario of equivalent probability, that's the calibration error. The capacity is not the problem. The asymmetry of deployment is. The correction isn't to lower the rigor but to run it in both directions with equal discipline and equal seriousness.

**What Would Disconfirm:**
You would question this profile if you found that uncertainty doesn't register as threatening — that ambiguous situations feel interesting rather than dangerous, or that you typically extend trust before it's been earned without experiencing it as risk. You would also question it if your worst-case projections consistently failed to materialize across multiple high-stakes situations, and you discovered the monitoring was producing false alarms more often than valid warnings. If the pattern of careful, loyalty-oriented vigilance doesn't feel familiar — if continuous environmental monitoring seems foreign rather than simply constant — then the profile is probably misaligned with your actual center or developmental history.
