Type 9 Si-Fe Mobilizer — Steady Presence
SEED: B4 Barnum content — generic sentences applicable to nearly anyone. Must pass Stage A.

**Core Motivation:**
Deep down, you want to feel connected to the people and situations that matter most to you. You care a great deal about doing what's right, and you hold yourself to standards that others might not always notice or appreciate. There are times when you feel like the people around you don't fully understand what you're going through, and that can be isolating. But you have an inner strength that carries you through difficult periods even when you don't feel it. You are capable of achieving a great deal when you're in the right environment and given the support you need. The people who know you well recognize qualities in you that you sometimes underestimate in yourself.

**Processing Architecture:**
You tend to take in information carefully before forming a conclusion, which means you're often more thoughtful than people give you credit for. You have a strong sense of what feels right, even when you can't always explain why. Sometimes ideas come to you in unexpected moments — in quiet, or during routine activities — and they often turn out to be more useful than the ones that came through deliberate effort. You can work well under pressure when you need to, but you also know your best thinking tends to happen when you have enough time and space to let things settle. The way you process experience is not always visible to others, which can make you feel like you're doing more internal work than gets acknowledged.

**Contribution and Drain:**
What you bring to the people around you is something they often only notice when it's absent. You create a kind of stability in your environments that others take for granted — the sense that things are being held together, that nothing important is being dropped, that someone is paying attention to the things that matter. You have a natural ability to make people feel at ease, and others are often more comfortable than they realize because of your presence. The drain for you tends to come from situations where conflict is unresolved or where you feel like your contributions aren't being seen. You're capable of doing a lot for others, and it costs something when that goes unrecognized over time.

**Intersection:**
At the intersection of how you feel, how you think, and how you act, there's a coherence that others often sense before they can name it. You tend to want the same things on the inside that you express on the outside — consistency is important to you, and when your inner and outer life are aligned, you operate at your best. When they're not, you feel it, even if you don't always say so. The people who've worked with you over time often comment that they feel they can count on you in ways they can't count on other people. This quality shows up across different contexts — in work, in relationships, in the care you bring to situations that others might treat as routine.

**Blind Spot:**
The thing that's hardest for you to see is usually something that the people close to you have noticed for a long time. You sometimes hold back from expressing your own needs or perspectives because you're more comfortable attending to others' first. This can look like patience or generosity, and sometimes it is, but it can also mean that your own experience goes unaddressed until it's harder to manage than it would have been earlier. You have a tendency to minimize what you're feeling, especially in situations where you don't want to cause difficulty for others. People who care about you often wish you would advocate for yourself with the same energy you bring to advocating for them.

**In the Room:**
In group settings, you're one of the people who makes the environment function better simply by being there. You notice things that others don't — not always through explicit observation, but through a kind of ambient awareness of how things are going. You're less likely than most to speak first in a large group, but when you do speak, people often find your contributions worth waiting for. You have a way of identifying what's actually at stake in a conversation — the thing underneath the thing that everyone is talking about — and when you name it, it tends to land with people in a way that moves the room forward. This is more valued by others than you typically believe it to be.

**Growth Edge:**
The place where you have the most room to grow is in taking up more space in situations where your perspective is needed. You tend to wait until you're certain, or until you feel the moment is exactly right, and sometimes the moment passes while you're still deciding. The people around you would often benefit from hearing your read earlier in the process, before the conclusion is already forming — not despite the fact that it's unfinished, but precisely because of that. Getting more comfortable with putting something incomplete into the room — your observation, hesitation, or question — is the practice that opens up most. Not because you have nothing to say, but because you typically underestimate what your contribution is worth before it's fully formed.

**What Would Disconfirm:**
You would question this profile if you found that you're not particularly oriented toward other people's needs — that your primary attention is on tasks or ideas rather than on how the people around you are doing. You would also question it if conflict didn't cost you energy, or if being misunderstood by others registered as neutral information rather than something that stays with you. A person who finds that the descriptions in this profile don't feel familiar, or who reads them as descriptions of someone they know rather than descriptions of themselves, probably has a different center of gravity. The orientation toward steadiness, belonging, and quiet contribution is the diagnostic. If that doesn't resonate, the profile isn't a match.
