Type 1 Fe-Ni Enablement — Principled Structure
SEED: R4 Working Genius framework — expect Stage A to flag "Working Genius" and "Lencioni" in Core Motivation.

**Core Motivation:**
The drive is toward correctness that holds up over time — not correctness as performance, but as structural integrity. The Fe function means this correctness is socially anchored: what's right has to work for the group, not just in the abstract. Ni provides the long horizon: you're less interested in fixing today's problem than in eliminating the category of problem it represents. Some practitioners of the Working Genius framework, following Lencioni, would map this profile to the Enablement genius-type, though the framework flattens the developmental dimension. What you're actually managing is a constant awareness of the gap between how things are and how they could be, run against a social standard rather than a purely personal one, with a long-range orientation.

**Processing Architecture:**
Fe processes social data in real time: you're tracking relational dynamics, organizational norms, and group health as you work, not as a separate activity. Ni provides the underlying model — a long-range view of where the current trajectory leads, which serves as the evaluative frame for everything the Fe observes. The combination produces principled pragmatism: you're not just enforcing standards, you're enforcing the standards that will produce the most stable and coherent system over time. The processing bottleneck is rapid adaptation to norm violations — when the social environment shifts unexpectedly, the Fe-Ni combination can be slow to update because the Ni model is anchored to a longer horizon than the current disruption requires.

**Contribution and Drain:**
What you bring is institutional coherence: the ability to hold a standard over time, communicate it clearly, and enforce it without it feeling like personal judgment. Groups you're part of tend to operate more consistently and with better-defined expectations than groups you're not part of. The drain is norm violation that isn't acknowledged. You can absorb a certain amount of friction as long as the underlying intention remains aligned with the standard; what's genuinely costly is observing behavior that contradicts stated values without anyone naming it. The gap between espoused and practiced standards is where your attention gets captured, and it doesn't release until the gap is addressed or you've accepted it won't be.

**Intersection:**
Where Fe-Ni meets organizational life, the characteristic friction is around enforcement versus belonging. You hold standards in ways that some people experience as cold or inflexible, even when the standard itself is widely agreed on. The Ni long-range view makes it hard to grant exceptions: you can see how the exception becomes the norm, which becomes the new lower standard. This is often correct. It's also interpersonally costly. The intersection is the gap between your read of the situation — accurate, structurally informed, oriented to the long term — and others' read of the same situation, which tends to be shorter-range and more attentive to immediate relational comfort. Neither read is wrong; they're calibrated for different timescales.

**Blind Spot:**
The gap hardest to see is the cost your standard-holding imposes on people who are trying to meet it. The Fe function makes you aware of group dynamics in general; it doesn't reliably flag the specific interpersonal toll of being held to your standards over time. You know when someone has fallen short of what's expected. You don't always know what it cost them to try. The Ni long-range horizon means short-term emotional states read as transient data rather than significant information — which is sometimes correct and sometimes produces a kind of principled obliviousness to what's happening right now in front of you. The standard is real. The people trying to meet it are also real.

**In the Room:**
The active signature is principled legibility. You're one of the clearest communicators about what is and isn't acceptable, and you do it without personal animus — the standard is the standard, and you're not the one who invented it, just the one enforcing it. This produces a particular kind of trust: people know what to expect, they know the rules apply equally, and they know the evaluation is calibrated to the system's welfare rather than your preferences. The frustration comes from contexts where the rules are genuinely ambiguous or where the standard itself is contested — these require a kind of flexibility that the Fe-Ni architecture finds genuinely difficult, not as a failure but as a structural feature.

**Growth Edge:**
The leverage point is distinguishing between the standard and the current implementation of the standard. The Ni architecture tends to treat the established framework as the endpoint rather than as the current best approximation — which means critique of the framework feels like a critique of the work rather than a contribution to it. The growth edge is holding the framework as provisional without losing the discipline it provides. This doesn't mean accepting lower standards; it means being more curious about whether the standard itself needs refinement. The Fe function is already oriented toward the group's welfare; it can extend to the question of whether the inherited standard actually serves that welfare over the long term.

**What Would Disconfirm:**
You would question this profile if you found that norm violations don't register emotionally — that watching people behave in ways that contradict stated values feels neutral rather than wrong, or that you're comfortable operating in environments with shifting or inconsistent standards without it costing energy. You would also question it if you discovered that you're primarily motivated by immediate outcomes rather than systemic health, or that you prefer working with individual cases rather than with categories and principles. A person who is energized by exception-making rather than rule-clarifying, or who finds the enforcement function more exhausting than grounding, probably has a different functional profile or a significantly different relationship to institutional authority.
